## CORRECTION 2026-08-25 -- G1 RANKED ON THE WRONG AXIS
================================================================================

G1 concluded "NO GOLDILOCKS POINT in 6-18" because the MARGIN
(best off-target minus on-target) rises monotonically with length. That
conclusion is about RELATIVE specificity and it is not the quantity that
governs off-target burden at a therapeutic dose.

Checked against the published literature on this exact drug and dataset
(Ottesen et al., NAR Molecular Medicine 2026, the paper behind GSE307431):
"Shorter ASOs tend to have FEWER hybridization-dependent off-target effects
than longer ASOs sharing the same target due to low tolerance for mismatch
base pairing", and MOE off-target skipping is "mainly due to tolerance for
mismatch base pairing with ISS-N1-like motifs".

MECHANISM WE MISSED: a longer ASO can afford more mismatches and still form a
stable duplex. An 18-mer with 4 mismatches retains 14 matched bases; a 10-mer
with 4 mismatches retains 6 and does not bind.

OUR OWN AXIS-2 TABLE SHOWS IT, in a column the original analysis read past:

  Spearman(length, ABSOLUTE best off-target affinity) = -0.700  p = 0.0165
  Spearman(length, relative MARGIN)                   = +0.618  p = 0.0426

Both significant, OPPOSITE directions, same eleven rows. Best off-target
binding strengthens from -9.26 kcal/mol at 6 nt to -17.49 at 18 nt (-8.23).
POLR2H specifically goes -5.83 -> -17.11 (-11.28).

AXIS 1 HAS THE SAME BLIND SPOT. It counted EXACT and 1-MISMATCH sites, where
the 18-mer scores perfectly (2 exact, 0 one-mismatch). POLR2H -- the validated
off-target, confirmed at dPSI -0.920 -- is a FOUR-MISMATCH site. The uniqueness
axis never searched the mismatch depth where the real off-targets live.

WHAT STANDS: the 18-mer is the most UNIQUE length at exact match, and it has
the best RELATIVE margin. Both are true and neither predicts off-target burden.

WHAT DOES NOT STAND: "no Goldilocks point" as a statement about ASO safety, and
any use of G1 to argue that shortening carries no specificity benefit. On the
absolute axis, shortening looks BETTER, which is what the literature reports.

CONSEQUENCE FOR ARM W: F14 and F10 are predicted to carry LOWER off-target
burden than F18, and that prediction is NOT testable by western blot --
off-targets are in other genes, absent from the construct. It needs RNA-seq.
