The rule predicts '+' for 98.1% of cells. It is a NEAR-CONSTANT PREDICTOR, and
that is why its accuracy (29.6%) simply tracks the '+' rate (27.7%). The
suspicion recorded in docs/null-postmortem.md was correct: it is NOT
anti-correlated, it just almost never fires.

BUT WHEN IT FIRES IT IS NEVER WRONG: 77/77, zero false negatives.
Fisher exact on the 2x2: odds ratio infinite, p = 1.76e-11.

HONEST READING, primary and secondary kept separate:
  PRIMARY (pre-registered): accuracy vs majority-class base rate -- NULL.
  SECONDARY: a downstream share >= 90% is a SUFFICIENT condition for a
    negative sign, on the pre-declared threshold, never violated in 3975 cells.

So the Arm N mechanism is REAL BUT NARROW. Driving the 5'ss's partner
downstream does force a negative sign -- it just happens in only 1.9% of cells
(77/3975), so it explains essentially none of the 87% sign-flip problem. The
hypothesis was right about direction and wrong about scope.

This does NOT rescue Arm N. The arm was proposed to explain the span-dependence
and it explains 4.3% of flipping footprints. It is still a failed post-hoc
lead; it is now a failed post-hoc lead with a narrow true core.
