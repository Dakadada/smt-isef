# STOP–GO audit of public steric splice-intervention datasets

## 1. Method note

**Audit date:** 2026-08-28. **Scope:** public experiments in which a sequence-programmed trans effector binds an unchanged pre-mRNA and an endpoint splicing outcome is measured. Cis variants, RBP knockdown, active RNA cleavage, DNA editing, small-molecule splice modulation, and intervention papers without public raw reads were not counted as labels.

I searched NCBI GEO/SRA/BioProject, ENA/ArrayExpress leads, PubMed/PMC, and primary-paper data-availability statements with combinations of `antisense`, `ASO`, `SSO`, `splice switching`, `morpholino`, `PMO`, `U7`, `snRNA`, `dCas13`, `dCasRx`, `RNA-seq`, and the loci `SMN2`, `DMD`, `STMN2`, `UNC13A`, and `FMR1`. Concrete lookups included GEO pages for GSE307431, GSE167762, GSE221900, GSE202178, and GSE235673; the Recinos et al. paper and its PRJNA1052769 data statement; and the Domenger et al. paper and its PRJEB13980 deposition statement. The searchable web service was available and returned those records. Direct command-line archive access was not: the attempted command

```text
curl -L --max-time 20 -sS 'https://www.ebi.ac.uk/ena/portal/api/search?result=read_run&query=study_accession%3D%22PRJNA1052769%22&fields=study_accession,secondary_study_accession,run_accession,sample_accession,experiment_title,library_strategy,library_source,library_selection,fastq_ftp,fastq_md5&format=tsv&limit=0'
```

failed with `curl: (6) Could not resolve host: www.ebi.ac.uk`. Neither `prefetch` nor `fasterq-dump` is installed. Consequently, **no FASTQ was downloaded in this audit**. “Archive-confirmed” below means an actual GEO page said “Raw data are available in SRA,” or a primary paper explicitly identified raw FASTQ/Illumina reads and an archive accession. It does **not** mean byte-level retrieval succeeded. Every series still needs `prefetch --max-size u <SRR>` or ENA FASTQ-URL/MD5 verification in a networked environment before model use.

Primary records used for verification: [GSE307431](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/query/acc.cgi?acc=GSE307431), [GSE167762](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/query/acc.cgi?acc=GSE167762), [GSE221900](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/query/acc.cgi?acc=GSE221900), [Recinos et al. / PRJNA1052769](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11076525/), and [Domenger et al. / PRJEB13980](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5785776/). The last paper explicitly says its demultiplexed raw data were stored as FASTQ at ENA PRJEB13980.

### Eligibility rule used for the counts

A study family is “usable” only if it has (1) a steric trans intervention, (2) treated and control endpoint samples, (3) public raw reads declared by the archive or primary paper, and (4) enough metadata to identify the intervention. Exact sequence remains a required pre-training field; where it could not be transcribed from a machine-readable source during this pass it is marked **to verify**, not guessed. Pooled guide-enrichment sequencing is not treated as a splice endpoint: only individually treated RNA-seq or targeted splice-product sequencing counts as an intervention label.

## 3. Deduplication notes

- **GSE307431 is not Ottesen et al. 2021 / PMCID PMC8395096.** The live GEO record lists a 2025 submission/2026 release, 20 libraries, three chemistries at 6 µM, and contributors Ottesen, Murzyn, Kaas, Bertrand, Payne, and Singh. The 2021 paper is a different low-dose Anti-N1 experiment and no public raw archive was verified. The earlier catalog’s suggestion that these might be aliases is therefore rejected.
- **GSE167762, SRP308402, and PRJNA705187 are aliases for one study family**, not three datasets.
- **PRJNA1052769 contains two assay domains.** The 1,937-guide pooled screen and the four-library individual-guide RNA-seq experiment are one primary family. The pooled screen does not contribute 1,937 endpoint labels under this audit’s rule.
- **Nusinersen-like ISS-N1 interventions recur across GSE307431, GSE167762, GSE221900, and earlier Ottesen work.** Repetition across studies helps reproducibility but does not create independent target loci or independent oligo sequences. Chemistry-distinct F18OMe/F18MOE/F20PMO are counted as three intervention implementations within GSE307431, not three study families.
- **PRJEB13980 is the material first-pass miss.** It is an independent DMD/U7-snRNA family and overturns the claim that all retrievable intervention studies are SMN2/ISS-N1, subject to final ENA run/FASTQ verification.

## 4. Cis-only prior datasets

These are explicitly **not usable as trans labels**:

| Dataset/resource | Why it is cis/prior-only | Permitted role |
|---|---|---|
| GTEx RNA-seq + genotypes/sQTLs | Natural sequence/genotype and tissue differences; no steric effector | Pretrain tissue/splice priors; never label ASO/dCas outcome |
| ClinVar splice variants with RNA evidence | Disease-associated cis sequence changes | Variant baseline/evaluation only |
| MPSA collections (Vex-seq, MaPSy, MFASS, saturation assays) | Reporter or endogenous cis mutagenesis | Cis splicing representation/pretraining only |
| GSE121569 and related STMN2 TDP-43-loss data | RBP depletion/disease state reveals the cryptic event but is not an ASO/dCas intervention | Event definition and cellular-context prior |
| PRJEB42763, GSE126543, GSE182976 and related UNC13A/TDP-43 datasets | TDP-43 depletion, disease, or amplicon characterization rather than steric treatment | Event/context prior only |
| GSE235673 | DNA base editing changes the transcript’s cis template | Excluded from trans task; possible cis comparator |
| GSE221868 MPSA arm | Cis reporter sequence library under small-molecule splice modulation; neither sequence-programmed steric trans intervention nor unchanged-transcript label | Mechanistic prior only |

## 5. Feasibility gate counts

Counts below are **provisional until run-level prefetch and exact-sequence transcription are completed**. They use the four archive-anchored primary families that satisfy the conceptual inclusion rule: GSE307431, GSE167762, PRJNA1052769 individual-guide RNA-seq, and PRJEB13980. GSE221900 is not counted because it is an uncontrolled longitudinal clinical/context study.

- **Independent primary TRANS study families with usable raw reads: N = 0 strictly verified here; N = 4 archive-anchored candidates** (GSE307431, GSE167762, PRJNA1052769 individual-guide RNA-seq, and PRJEB13980). The strict count is zero because no run could be resolved and fetched in this environment; all four require run-level retrieval verification. The archive-evidence count is reported separately so the network limitation is not mistaken for evidence that the studies do not exist.
- **Independent loci: N = 2** (SMN2 and DMD).
- **Independent oligo/guide-level interventions: N = 5 provisionally**: three chemistry/length-distinct ISS-N1 ASOs in GSE307431, one dCas13d guide D[333,357], and one U7-E53 cassette. GSE167762 repeats the nusinersen/ISS-N1 intervention and does not add a unique molecule; the pooled 1,937-guide screen is excluded because its deposited screen reads are guide enrichment rather than endpoint splice reads. Because several exact strings remain to be transcribed, the **strict sequence-verified count today is N = 0**.
- **Distinct effector classes: N = 5** when chemistry/mechanism is preserved as required by the proposed benchmark: 2′-OMe-PS ASO, 2′-MOE-PS ASO, PMO, dCas13d RNP, and U7 snRNP. At a coarser modality level these collapse to three broad classes (free ASO, dCas13d RNP, U7 snRNP), but the reported gate count is five.

Can the present public set support the proposed workflow?

- **(a) Adapter-training pool: No.** Five unique endpoint interventions are not a trainable intervention pool, and three are same-site chemistry variants. The large dCas13 screen cannot rescue this count without changing the endpoint definition to a fluorescence/guide-enrichment proxy.
- **(b) Study-level nested tuning: No.** Four families exist provisionally, but two free-ASO families are the same SMN2/ISS-N1 molecule/site, one dCas family contributes one RNA-seq guide, and the only second locus contributes one U7 cassette with a vector-control confound. Nested study-level hyperparameter selection would be unstable and biologically non-identifiable.
- **(c) Leave-study-out validation: No for a general benchmark.** A mechanical four-fold rotation is possible, but each fold would largely be leave-effector/locus-out extrapolation from only one or a few interventions, not a meaningful estimate of generalization. It may be reported descriptively in a feasibility study, not as a benchmark performance claim.
- **(d) One never-touched terminal study: No.** Reserving one of four leaves at most three tiny, highly dependent families for every training/tuning decision; reserving PRJEB13980 removes the only DMD locus, while reserving any SMN2 family leaves inadequate intervention diversity.

The numerical gate’s minimum-family and minimum-locus clauses are provisionally met (4 ≥ 3 families; 2 ≥ 2 loci), but its conjunctive requirements of **enough independent interventions** and **one trainable pool plus one terminal study** are not met. Archive-level existence is also not yet equivalent to verified retrievability: all four families remain pending a successful run-table export and at least one `prefetch`/FASTQ integrity check per run.

## 6. Final verdict: NO-GO

**NO-GO — narrow the project to an SMN2 case study plus a public-data feasibility/negative-result paper.** The broad audit overturns the narrow factual claim that only two SMN2 datasets exist: it found **4 provisional primary archive-anchored families across 2 loci and 3 broad effector classes**, including the missed DMD U7-snRNA dataset PRJEB13980 and the individual-guide dCas13d RNA-seq arm of PRJNA1052769. The strict retrievability count in this environment is **0 successfully fetched families**, however, and the biological counts remain only **5 provisional unique endpoint interventions, 0 exact-sequence-and-FASTQ-byte-verified interventions, no trainable adapter pool, and no defensible terminal study**. Thus the literal ≥3-family/≥2-locus floor is met only at archive-evidence level, while the full conjunctive gate is not. The honest publishable result is that public raw intervention data are broader than the first pass suggested but remain far too sparse and dependent for a general steric-splicing benchmark.

## 7. Retrievability verification (2026-08-28, networked orchestration session)

The DNS/toolkit block was specific to the Codex sandbox. The main session HAS outbound network; ENA REST `filereport` (metadata only, no FASTQ downloaded) confirms all four candidate families are byte-retrievable:

| Project | result=read_run runs | runs with `fastq_ftp` | library_strategy |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| PRJNA1321660 (GSE307431) | 20 | 20 | RNA-Seq |
| PRJNA705187 (GSE167762) | 28 | 28 | RNA-Seq + ChIP-Seq + OTHER (RNA-seq subset is the endpoint) |
| PRJNA1052769 (dCas13d) | 16 | 16 | RNA-Seq + AMPLICON (RNA-seq subset = individual-guide endpoint; amplicon = pooled screen) |
| PRJEB13980 (DMD/U7) | 12 | 12 | RNA-Seq |

**Effect on the verdict:** retrievability is no longer the blocker — the reads exist and are fetchable. The binding constraint is unchanged and remains decisive: ~5 unique interventions, 3 of them the same SMN2/ISS-N1 site, across only 2 loci, with a vector-control confound on the sole DMD family. Verdict stays **NO-GO for a powered general benchmark**, but the achievable scope is upgraded from "SMN2 only" to **SMN2 core + a limited 2-locus / multi-effector (ASO×3 chemistries + dCas13d + U7) FEASIBILITY/transportability benchmark**, reported as feasibility, not a generalization performance claim. Exact-sequence transcription per intervention and one `prefetch`/MD5 integrity check per run are still required before model use.
