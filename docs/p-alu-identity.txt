### Exploratory follow-up, labeled as such
The composite score above weights six hallmarks equally. The single most
Alu-diagnostic component is the Pol III internal promoter: a PERFECT A-box
(0 mismatches) AND a PERFECT B-box (0 mismatches) at canonical spacing. Those
motifs and bounds were declared in the module before any run; what is post-hoc
is looking at that triad on its own, so this is reported as EXPLORATORY and
does not change the verdict.

Frequency of the same perfect triad in 3000 null draws (composition-matched
shuffles + random same-length intron windows), scored in both orientations:

So one arm of hit 1 carries a perfect Pol III promoter that occurs in 0.6% of
null sequence -- strong single-arm evidence. Its PARTNER does not (A-box mm=1,
B-box mm=1, spacing OK). The both-arms requirement therefore still fails on the
strictest diagnostic, which is why the verdict stands.

### What changed from the first run, and why it matters
The first run reported hit 1 upstream at z = -0.54, "indistinguishable from
null", and concluded the pair was not Alu. That was a STRAND BUG: hallmarks()
scanned one orientation only, while an inverted-repeat pair has one arm
antisense by construction. Three of six hallmarks are strand-specific and a 3'
poly-A tail reads as a 5' poly-T head when reversed, so the inverted arm was
guaranteed to score near null regardless of what it was.

Corrected, that arm scores z = +2.57 with a PERFECT A-box and B-box at correct
spacing. The two arms of hit 1 are now symmetric (+2.57 / +2.41) rather than
one being null -- which is what an inverted Alu pair should look like.

Both still fall below the pre-declared calling threshold of z = 2.58. The
threshold was NOT moved after seeing this. Verdict is unchanged in name and
much better supported in substance: Alu-scale inverted repeats, subfamily
unresolved, with hit 1 the strongest candidate by a clear margin.
