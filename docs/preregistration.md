# Pre-registration — locked before any PSI exists

**Committed 2026-08-23.** Nothing in this file may be edited after the wet-lab
data arrives. If a prediction here turns out wrong, it stays in the record with
the outcome appended below it, exactly as written.

## Why this file exists

The framework's link function `A_5ss -> PSI` is deliberately unfit — no real
PSI exists yet, and fitting a link to synthetic data would be worthless. That
is the correct call, but it means the central claim is currently **untested**,
not supported, and a reviewer is right to say so.

Pre-registration answers that without any PSI: the predictions are written
down, committed and timestamped *before* the measurement exists, so they cannot
be adjusted afterwards. What follows is deliberately the project's own weakest
point rather than its strongest.

## P1 (primary) — span robustness is a real design criterion

A7 found that **87.0% of footprints flip the sign of their predicted effect**
across the `max_bp_span` ladder, and that this is *not* a near-zero-noise
artifact: the flip rate is 85.7% even among the largest-magnitude footprints
(`smt.validate.span_robustness`, H-noise refuted). The robust minority is,
however, significantly clustered in position at all four targets (p = 0.0005,
2000 permutations).

The framework therefore claims span robustness carries real information.
That is falsifiable:

> Take pairs of footprints **matched on best-span |fom|** — so a single-span
> analysis ranks them equally — and on length exactly, where one holds its
> sign across the whole ladder and the other does not. **The span-robust
> member suppresses the cryptic exon more than its partner.**

**Null:** the two members are indistinguishable. If the null holds, span
robustness carries no information, worst-span ranking was a mistake, and A7's
candidate list should not be used.

This is a strong test because the naive single-span analysis — what the field
would actually do — predicts **no difference within a pair, by construction**.
Only the ladder-based framework predicts one.

**Panel:** `docs/prereg-panel.csv`, 40 oligos, 20 pairs across all four
targets, generated by `smt.validate.prereg_panel`. Pairs are >= 25 nt apart so
they interrogate independent positions, and the two members of a pair are
>= 10 nt apart so they are distinguishable oligos.

**Decision rule, fixed now:**
- Primary: paired **Wilcoxon signed-rank** on the *magnitude* of the
  within-pair difference in cryptic-exon PSI, one-sided, alpha = 0.05.
- A pair counts only if both members are measurable above the assay's noise
  floor, **declared by the wet lab before unblinding**.
- Tiering was declared before generation: a pair is PRIMARY only if the robust
  member's weakest span retains >= 1% of its own best-span magnitude. Sign
  consistency at |fom| ~ 1e-7 is the framework predicting *no* effect, so
  expecting it to win would test a prediction never made.

**Power, computed before measurement and stated honestly: only 4 of 20 pairs
are PRIMARY.** A one-sided sign test on 4 pairs cannot reach p < 0.05 even if
every pair goes the predicted way (min p = 0.0625). This is why the primary
readout is graded (Wilcoxon on effect size over all 20 pairs) rather than a
sign test on 4. **If the wet lab can only return direction, not magnitude,
P1 is underpowered and must be reported as such rather than claimed.**

## P2 (secondary) — the STMN2 candidate

The strongest ladder-robust candidate is **STMN2 start 5716, length 7–10**
(`fom` +6.7e-03 at its weakest span, 94.3% of that locus's ceiling), part of a
735-footprint robust set clustered at p = 0.0005.

> Predicted: an ASO tiling 5716+10 (`5'-GAAGGUCUUC-3'`) reduces STMN2 cryptic
> exon inclusion relative to a scrambled control of equal length and GC content.

**This is the weaker prediction of the two** and should not be presented as the
headline. Many things reduce cryptic exon inclusion; a hit here is consistent
with the framework but does not discriminate it from simpler explanations. P1
discriminates; P2 does not.

## P3 — what would falsify the framework outright

Stated now so it cannot be renegotiated later:

1. **P1 null holds with adequate power** (>= 12 usable pairs, graded readout):
   span robustness carries no information. The `max_bp_span` ladder is then
   just noise and A7's ranking is unjustified.
2. **Direct-occupancy ASOs match or beat the structural predictions.** The
   framework models *indirect* structural effects only and refuses footprints
   overlapping their own readout. If direct occupancy explains the data equally
   well, the indirect mechanism is superfluous.
3. **The measured PSI ordering is uncorrelated with `A_5ss` at every span.**
   Not "weakly correlated" — the sieve already failed at rho = 0.0025 and that
   is in the record. A null here too would mean the observable does not track
   splicing at the loci it was designed for.

## Standing negatives that are NOT up for renegotiation

These are already established and must survive into any write-up:

- The sieve does **not** work transcriptome-wide: Spearman rho = 0.0025,
  p = 0.96, sign agreement 56.7%. "Universal safety sieve" is unsupported.
- Its four validated hits show 10.1x tail enrichment at |dPSI| >= 0.8
  (raw p = 0.020) but **nothing survives Bonferroni over 24 tests**. The
  hypothesis is three measurable genes short of testable
  (`docs/a5-enrichment-test.txt`).
- 87.0% span sign-flipping is real and is not a noise artifact.
- A6 recovered intron 7 blind (1.000) but **not** intron 3 (0.421) — 1 of 2.
- R3's SMN2 dPSI +0.368 recovers a fact already known from nusinersen's
  approval. It validates the pipeline; it is not a discovery.

# Arm V — independent replication + dose axis (Ottesen 2021)

**Committed 2026-08-24, before any enrichment, correlation, or sign-agreement
number was computed on this dataset.** Only DESIGN facts were established first
(n per category, event counts, the ∆PSI sign convention, the control design);
no relationship between predicted and measured effects has been evaluated.

## The dataset, and why it is genuine replication

Ottesen, Luo, Singh & Singh 2021, *IJMS* 22(16):8378 (PMC8395096), CC BY.
Supplementary tables fetched via the Europe PMC REST endpoint (PMC's own
download path serves a JS interstitial and the NCBI OA package path returns
404). Pinned: `PMC8395096_supplementary.zip`, 4,904,378 bytes, sha256
`3fc782cb933efac189167efa3c4379763ce8dc0c681bdf5bfb63aca0a2ffc2a0`; inner
`ijms-22-08378-s001.zip` sha256
`a66a01532be2b09f3804ad69f4d22b9ed2ecf87af606140b724e2514897130b3`.

Every sieve grade this project has ever produced came from GSE307431 alone,
which is why `docs/a5-enrichment-pooled.txt` had to write "two groupings of ONE
dataset are not independent replication, so the denominator is 36, not 12."
This is a **different experiment** — different ASO doses, different design —
in the **same cell line** (GM03813, the SMN1-null line R3 already characterised).

**Design facts, established before any prediction was scored:**
- 4,300 EIN + ESK event rows; **4,183 carry both doses**; 2,644 unique genes.
- **123 sieve-predicted genes have a measurable event**, of which
  **exonic_proximal = 18** (also intronic_core 46, three_utr 33, exonic_core 31,
  intronic_proximal 5).
- Attack 1 specified the gate: *"the test clears the corrected threshold at
  n = 14 predicted genes with a measurable event. Eleven exist."* **n = 18.
  The hypothesis is testable for the first time.** The threshold was declared
  before this dataset was opened and is NOT moved here.
- Control design: `10mer` is a **non-targeting control ASO** with no effect on
  SMN2 exon 7 splicing. LoA (5 nM Anti-N1) was **topped up to 100 nM total
  oligo with the 10mer**, so sequence-independent oligo load is held constant
  across doses. The dose contrast is purely sequence-dependent. This is the
  authors' design, not an assumption of ours.

## SIGN CONVENTION — verified arithmetically, and it is INVERTED

The published ∆PSI is **control − treated**, the opposite of this project's
convention everywhere else (R3 uses `dPSI = F18MOE − ScrMOE`; rMATS uses
treated − control). Checked on all 4,286 rows carrying the three columns:

    dPSI == control - treated :  100.0% within 1e-3 (max residual 0.0003 = rounding)
    dPSI == treated - control :    0.0% within 5e-3 (median residual 0.396)

Independently confirmed semantically: in the exon-INCLUSION sheet a negative
∆PSI means treated PSI exceeded control PSI, i.e. inclusion rose — which is what
that sheet is supposed to contain.

**All V analyses therefore use `dPSI_project = −(published ∆PSI)`.** Comparing
signed predictions to the published column unflipped would invert every sign
conclusion silently — the same class as P's strand bug and R3's SMN1/SMN2
tie-break. A test asserts the convention so it cannot quietly stop being true.

## V1 (primary) — does the tail enrichment replicate?

Mirrors `smt.sieve.enrichment` exactly: the same categories, the same |dPSI|
thresholds (0.2 / 0.5 / 0.8), one-sided Fisher, **base rate printed first**,
**Bonferroni over every test printed including those that do not support the
hypothesis**.

> **Primary cell:** `exonic_proximal` at |dPSI| >= 0.2 — the cell that gave
> 5.97x, raw p = 0.0007, corrected p = 0.0253 on GSE307431. Predicted to
> replicate: enrichment above base rate, surviving Bonferroni at alpha = 0.05.

**Null:** nothing survives correction. Then the tail enrichment does not
replicate in an independent experiment, the GSE307431 result is a
single-dataset artifact, and the sieve's candidate-generator claim is weakened
rather than supported. **This is a real risk and the null is publishable.**

## V2 (the new statistic) — is dose sensitivity affinity-ordered?

The base-rate confound that hollowed out every previous sieve result — ~55% of
significant events are negative, so "predict skipping" wins that often by
chance — **does not apply to a dose contrast. Chance does not produce
affinity-ordered dose sensitivity.**

> Among sieve-predicted genes with an event at `|dPSI_HiA| >= 0.1` (so the
> ratio is defined), the dose-retention ratio `r = dPSI_LoA / dPSI_HiA` is
> **negatively rank-correlated with `ddG_net`** — tighter predicted binding
> (more negative `ddG_net`) retains more of its effect at 5 nM.
> Spearman, one-sided, alpha = 0.05.

**Null:** rho indistinguishable from 0. Dose sensitivity carries no information
about predicted affinity, and the hybridization model behind the sieve is not
doing the work its name claims.

Stated against interest: `r` is a ratio of two noisy quantities and is
heavy-tailed. The `|dPSI_HiA| >= 0.1` floor is declared **now**, not after
seeing the spread, and the count of events excluded by it is reported.

## V3 — do the four individually-validated hits replicate?

The one surviving claim from GSE307431 is that four strong exonic predictions
were individually confirmed (POLR2H −0.920, MAP1S −0.879, ANKRD28 −0.619,
CCSER2 −0.236). That claim is directly testable here.

> Predicted: of the four, those present in this dataset show `dPSI_project`
> in the **same direction** as in GSE307431.

**Null / limits stated up front:** n = 4 at most, and fewer if some are absent.
A sign test on 4 cannot reach p < 0.05 even if all four agree (min p = 0.0625).
**V3 is therefore reported as a descriptive replication check, never as a
significance claim**, and its absence from the multiple-testing denominator is
because no p-value is computed for it — not because it was excluded for
convenience.

## What Arm V may NOT be used to claim

- It cannot rehabilitate "universal safety sieve." The standing negative
  (rho = 0.0025 transcriptome-wide) is not up for renegotiation and is not
  re-tested here.
- The best available outcome is: **a dose-validated candidate generator for
  strong exonic off-targets, replicated in an independent experiment.**
- These are published summary tables, not raw reads. No re-alignment was done
  and the authors' event calling is taken as given, including their FDR.

# Arm M — the mechanism of A3, registered before any BPPM diff is computed

**Committed 2026-08-25.** A3 recovered the GC-core 8-mer blind and has never
been explained. C4 made the gap sharp: the exon 7 5'ss is held by a LOCAL
UPSTREAM hairpin (dominant partners ref 32030-32042, p ~ 0.99 at -10..-12,
inside exon 7), while GC_CORE sits 8 nt DOWNSTREAM and does not touch that
hairpin -- yet occluding it raises A_5ss 6-7x.

## M1 (primary) — the competition mechanism

> Occluding GC_CORE **lowers** the pairing probability between the 5'ss
> (32050-32058) and its dominant upstream partner block (32030-32042), because
> GC_CORE's own partners overlap that block and releasing them lets the block
> re-pair elsewhere. Declared direction: `P(5'ss : 32030-32042)` DECREASES
> under GC_CORE occlusion, at the worst span.

**Null:** the 5'ss-partner pairing is unchanged (|dP| < 0.05 at worst span).
Then the GC-core effect is not partner competition, A3 remains an empirical hit
with no mechanism, and the write-up says so. **Per the standing stopping rule,
Arms U and E do not then run.**

## M2 — the ISS-N1 contrast, which is the same question inverted

C4 measured ISS_N1 as 77.0% DOWNSTREAM-paired and GC_CORE as 76.3% UPSTREAM-
paired.

> Predicted: occluding full-length ISS_N1 does NOT produce the same decrease at
> `P(5'ss : 32030-32042)`, because its partners are downstream and do not
> compete for that block. This is the candidate explanation for the project's
> oldest open question -- why the full silencer inverts while the 8-mer works.

## M3 — the generalisation test that makes M1 useful rather than anecdotal

> Across all 780 intronic A3 footprints, the ones whose own partner set
> overlaps 32030-32042 are enriched among the span-robust top ranks relative to
> those whose partners do not. One-sided, alpha = 0.05.

**Null:** partner overlap does not predict rank. Then M1, even if true for
GC_CORE specifically, does not generalise and gives no map of where the
framework applies -- which was the whole point of asking.

## Declared before running
- Ranking and all reported dP are at the **worst span** over (50,100,150,300,0).
- The partner block 32030-32042 is taken from C4's measurement, which was made
  before this prediction and for a different purpose.
- BPPM is exact for per-base and per-pair probabilities; no independence
  approximation enters M1 or M2.
